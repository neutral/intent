# Author a Discipline

A Discipline offers a practice a worker can choose and adapt in context. Explain
the recurring problem, why the advice helps under its assumptions, and how to
recognize when another approach is more suitable. Useful guidance transfers
judgment to situations its author did not enumerate.

Make the practice, applicability, and actionable guidance substantive enough to
use. Explain what the worker would do, the situations where doing it helps, and
the reasoning needed to adapt it. Name important exceptions and tradeoffs. Keep
the basis and limits of claims visible; a successful anecdote alone does not
establish a universal rule.

## Use the common shape

Follow the Discipline shape in the [Knowledge contract](../spec/KNOWLEDGE.md).
Practice states the advice; Applicability and Guidance explain when and how to
use it. These essential sections accept ordinary prose and subheadings. Add
Exclusions or Verification Guidance when useful; entry IDs are optional anchors.
Develop explanation, examples, and decision aids within that shape.
Verification Guidance remains advice without creating mandatory Checks or storing
results.

Choose the depth needed for a worker to recognize when the practice helps, explain
why, adapt it, and identify reasons to set it aside. Optional family guidance
offers questions to select, combine, adapt, or skip. These replaceable files add
no subtype, schema requirement, behavior, or tool effect. See the shared
[authoring guidance](../GUIDANCE.md).

Discipline cannot create product obligations, execution permission, required
sources, or mandatory verification. A practice that must be enforced belongs in
Product Knowledge and its Checks. Respect publisher ownership: local edits cannot
claim unchanged exact adoption of publisher bytes. Publishing local guidance uses
a local publisher and Pack with honest provenance. The
[Discipline contract](../spec/DISCIPLINES.md) owns publication and adoption rules.

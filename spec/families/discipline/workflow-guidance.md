# Explain workflow guidance

Use this optional approach when the Discipline helps a worker coordinate several
steps toward an outcome. Explain how the steps cooperate, which conditions change
the path, and how to resume or reconsider after interruption. A useful sequence
transfers enough understanding that the worker can adapt it to the situation.

## Develop the guidance

Identify the intended outcome and a recognizable starting situation. Explain
prerequisites, needed information, and assumptions about participants or the
environment. Keep the scope bounded enough that the worker can tell when this
practice begins, what it contributes, and when its purpose has been met.

Explain the contribution of each step. Describe what it learns or changes and how
that result supports later work. Distinguish necessary ordering from a convenient
presentation order. If steps can be skipped, repeated, or done independently,
explain the conditions that make those choices reasonable.

Make handoffs and decisions visible. Identify the information another participant
needs, how unresolved questions remain visible, and what a worker should recognize
before proceeding. Advice about review, publication, or outward effects does not
grant permission for them or establish a mandatory approval process by itself.

Describe partial progress, interruption, and recovery. Explain which observations
may have become stale, which effects need to be understood before repetition,
and how to identify a safe next step. Do not assume that resuming from a saved
step number is sufficient when the underlying situation has changed.

Explain completion and evaluation. Finishing the sequence may produce a proposal
or a reviewed artifact while its intended benefit remains unobserved. Keep those
outcomes distinct. Suggest useful signs that the practice helped and limits on
what a single use can establish.

## What richer information adds

“Plan, implement, review” leaves both the purpose and the handoffs implicit.
Richer advice can explain how the plan resolves the consequential uncertainties,
how implementation exposes findings that may change the plan, and what context
lets a reviewer assess the result. A worker then has reasons to revisit a step
instead of following the sequence mechanically.

A migration-planning practice may need to explain compatible intermediate states,
dependencies between participants, and recovery after a partial transition. A
small documentation-change practice may need only the source of meaning, a
focused edit, and review of the affected reading path. Do not force the same
ceremony on both situations.

Use an explained sequence, a decision-point table, or an interrupted-work example
where it improves understanding. Keep commands or tool references subordinate
to the practice's meaning; their presence does not make the Discipline executable.

## Judge the result

A worker should understand why the steps cooperate, recognize conditions that
change the path, and recover without guessing what earlier work established.
Remove procedural detail that adds no useful judgment, while retaining the
prerequisites and recovery information needed to follow the advice.

# Define a Check that requires human judgment

Use this optional approach when assessing the promise requires interpretation of
meaning, usefulness, experience, or consequences that a simple mechanical
observation cannot settle. Make the judgment understandable and contestable.
Naming a reviewer or asking whether something is “good” does not establish what
would count as satisfaction.

## Develop the criteria

Identify the promise and the relevant audience or situation. Explain the task,
available context, starting conditions, and material to examine. A technical
expert reviewing an explanation and a first-time user trying to act on it can
answer different questions. Choose the context that bears on the supported
Knowledge instead of claiming a universal reader experience.

Describe the basis of judgment. Identify the properties, outcomes, or observable
difficulties that distinguish acceptable from unacceptable results. Examples can
calibrate interpretation, but explain the underlying reason so the assessor can
handle cases that differ from those examples. Avoid numerical scoring whose
dimensions or meaning remain undefined.

Explain the evidence required by that basis. A prose review can examine whether
an instruction states a prerequisite; observing a user can reveal whether the
instruction supplied enough information for that user to complete the task.
Neither automatically establishes the other's claim. State what the proposed
assessment can support and what would require different evidence.

Describe uncertainty and disagreement. Identify missing context, unsuitable
participants, conflicting observations, or ambiguous criteria that would prevent
a justified conclusion. Do not invent consensus or treat an absent objection as
Pass. Explain when an examination has not occurred at all, and when an attempted
examination remains Indeterminate.

## What richer information adds

“The interface is easy to use” leaves audience, task, and success meaning unclear.
If the promise concerns a person recovering from a failed import, criteria can
describe the information available after failure, whether the person can identify
what was retained, and how they can choose a safe next action. Those observations
address the specific task more directly than a general satisfaction adjective.

“The guide is complete” could mean all required headings exist, all documented
steps match the implementation, or an intended reader can finish a task without
missing context. State which proposition is being assessed. A structural check
can establish the headings; it cannot by itself establish reader understanding.

A short scenario, a few contrasting excerpts, or an explanation of a difficult
judgment can give more useful calibration than a long checklist. Preserve room
for the assessor to explain a case the examples do not cover, while keeping the
criteria tied to the supported promise.

## Judge the result

Imagine an artifact that looks polished or passes an automated proxy while
leaving the intended reader unable to act. Identify the criterion and evidence
that would expose that failure, or state the remaining limit using the
[Check review guidance](../../guidance/check-review.md).

Use the [common Check guidance](../../guidance/check.md) and
[Check contract](../../spec/CHECKS.md). This optional approach adds no mandatory
review workflow or stored judgment result. Intent owns the definition;
implementation assessment and reporting remain outside it.
